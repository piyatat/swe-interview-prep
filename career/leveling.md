# Leveling and scope

Levels are **scope labels**, not years-of-experience counters. Calibrate stories and resume language to the bar you’re targeting.

## Signal cheat sheet (IC)

| Signal | Mid | Senior | Staff+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem size | Well-scoped features | Ambiguous multi-sprint problems | Ill-defined org/multi-team problems |
| Ownership | Feature / service slice | Product area or critical path | Domain or platform direction |
| Influence | Teammates | Cross-team; mentors others | Sets technical direction; unblocks orgs |
| Execution | Delivers with guidance | Plans + delivers independently | Creates leverage (systems, strategy, people) |
| Failure mode | Needs clarity | Can create clarity | Creates clarity for others |

Company ladders differ (L4/L5 vs E4/E5 vs “Senior”). Always ask recruiters for **leveling guide or peer titles**.

## How to self-calibrate

1. List last 12–24 months of work as **problem → your role → outcome**.  
2. Mark each for ambiguity, blast radius, and influence.  
3. Compare to the table — claim the level where **most** strong evidence sits.  
4. Gaps become the growth plan ([growth.md](growth.md)), not fiction on the resume.

## Phrasing for resumes and interviews

| Prefer | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| “Owned X end-to-end across A/B teams” | “Helped the team with X” |
| “Defined approach when requirements were unclear” | “Worked in agile” |
| “Mentored N engineers; raised review bar” | “Team player” |

## Interview implications

- **Under-leveling** → easier bar, less comp, slower growth  
- **Over-leveling** → harder loop; behavioral/system design expects broader scope  
- Practice **scope narration**: why this problem mattered beyond your ticket  

See [general/behavioral.md](../general/behavioral.md) and role pages under `roles/`.

## Checklist

- [ ] Target level agreed with yourself (and recruiter when in process)  
- [ ] 3 stories that prove that level’s scope  
- [ ] Resume title/bullets match claimed level  
- [ ] Honest gap list for the next level up
