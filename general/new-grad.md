# Intern and new-grad SWE loops

Campus / early-career loops are **coding-heavy**, often **no full system-design round**, and run on a **front-loaded calendar**. Distinct from mid-level [prep-plan.md](prep-plan.md) (design + staff stories) and from [online-assessments.md](online-assessments.md) (OA mechanics only). Confirm mix with the recruiter — intern vs new-grad vs “early career L3/E3” is not identical.

## How this track differs

| vs mid-level / L4+ | Implication |
| --- | --- |
| Little or **no HLD** onsite (Meta E3 commonly coding + behavioral only) | Spend the design week on **CS fundamentals** and clean code, not Twitter-at-scale |
| **Two problems / 45 min** is common (phone and onsite) | Pace: leave ~18–20 min for problem 2 |
| **Team match** may be **after** hire (Google intern; Meta bootcamp) | Passing the loop ≠ picking the team |
| **Intern conversion** is a separate bar (project + host rec + headcount) | Treat the internship as a 12-week interview |

Peak application windows for **fall 2026 grads** opened mid-2025 at many big-tech employers; intern postings often open **mid-summer the year before** and close in weeks. Apply early; keep other processes live until a **written** offer.

## Typical shape (big tech)

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Resume / referral | Graduation date, projects, one modern language |
| Recruiter | Eligibility, locations, OA vs live, timeline |
| OA or take-home (sometimes) | Timed DSA; some Google intern paths: miss/fail coding sample → cooldown months |
| Phone / virtual screen | 1–2 coding problems; narrate; Google Doc or CoderPad |
| Virtual onsite | **2 coding** + **1 behavioral** is the common 2026 new-grad slate; Apple/Netflix sometimes add a domain round |
| Team match | Google intern: HM chats after technical pass; Meta new-grad: often **after** offer (bootcamp) |

Google intern technicals are often **2× 45 min** DSA; intern interviews may still use a **plain shared doc** (no run). Meta new-grad reports: phone **two problems in 45 min**, onsite **two coding + Jedi behavioral**, **no design at E3**.

## What they score (coding)

Same rubric as [coding-patterns.md](coding-patterns.md): clarify, brute → better, edges, complexity. Interviewers still want **you** to drive tests. AI policy is **stricter** on intern/new-grad classic rounds — assume **off** unless they say otherwise. See [ai-assisted-rounds.md](ai-assisted-rounds.md) only if the recruiter names a pilot.

## Behavioral without a long career

You do not need a Staff-shaped story bank. Prepare **4–6** short STARs:

- Ambiguity (underspecified class/project)
- Conflict (teammate / TA / intern host)
- Failure (bug, missed deadline) — [../answers/behavioral-failure.md](../answers/behavioral-failure.md)
- Learning speed (new stack in weeks)
- Impact (what **you** shipped, with a number)

Interns: the **host project** is the main story. “We” without “I” fails.

## Intern conversion (rough public picture)

Public estimates put return-offer rates **~60–80%** when the intern ships and the host writes a clear yes — still gated on **next-year headcount**. Student-researcher tracks may **not** convert the same way as SWE intern. Do not treat a strong intern loop as a guaranteed full-time offer.

## Prep emphasis (4–6 weeks)

- **Blind 75 / NeetCode 150 mediums**; timed **pairs**.
- Arrays, graphs, trees, heaps, two pointers — see [../answers/](../answers/) outlines.
- Light LLD / OOP if the company names it; skip celebrity-scale HLD unless they confirm.
- Recruiter questions: OA platform, language, AI, team-match **before or after** offer.

## Sample questions

1. “Walk me through a project on your resume — what did **you** implement?”
2. Two mediums back-to-back (hash map + BFS, or two pointers + tree).
3. “Tell me about a time you were stuck and how you unblocked.”
4. Team match: “What kind of code do you want to write for 12 weeks / first year?”
