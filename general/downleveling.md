# Downleveling (level vs hire)

**Downleveling** means you **pass the hire bar** but the packet is scoped **below the level you interviewed for** (e.g. targeting L5 / E5 / senior, offer is L4 / E4 / mid). It is usually a **committee or bar-raiser** call on **scope and impact**, not “you failed coding.” Mentioned briefly in [../companies/faang-big-tech.md](../companies/faang-big-tech.md); this page is the playbook.

Level is **not** the same as salary negotiation. Treat **level first**, then band, then start date.

## Why it happens

| Driver | What interviewers heard |
| --- | --- |
| Stories too local | “I implemented the ticket” vs owned outcome + org effect |
| Design stayed mid-level | APIs and boxes, no estimates, failure modes, or migration |
| Behavioral under-scoped | IC execution only when targeting staff |
| Split packet | Strong coding, mixed design → committee picks safer level |
| Calibration / market | Same skills map to a lower level at that company (Google L4 vs Meta E5 is a common mismatch) |
| Microsignals | Over-scripted STAR, never naming tradeoffs, “we” with no “I” |

Coding rarely sets level. **System design + behavioral + consistency across interviewers** do.

## Prevent it (before and during the loop)

1. **Ask the recruiter:** “What level is this loop scoped for?” If it is L−1, ask to interview at target or on the border **before** onsite.
2. **State target once** to HM / first interviewer: role level you are calibrating to — then **show** it; do not argue years of experience.
3. **Design at the level:** requirements, capacity, SLOs, evolution, what you would **not** build yet.
4. **STAR at the level:** your decision, stakeholders, metric, what you uniquely did. Staff stories need **cross-team / multi-quarter** scope.
5. **Same facts everywhere** — HC reads the packet; contradictions look junior.

## If the offer is downleveled

Do **not** accept, decline, or vent on the first call. Do **not** jump to sign-on or start date (that reads as accepting the level).

**Ask for diagnosis (24–48 h):**

> I’m interested in the team. I interviewed for [target]. What drove [offered] leveling — which rounds? Is there a path to extra signal or committee re-review?

Then, in order:

1. **Level** — extra design/behavioral interview, HM conversation, written scope brief of past work.
2. **Comp inside the band** — top-of-band + sign-on can close year-one cash; it does **not** restore title, promo clock, or scope.
3. **Paperwork** — only after you chose level vs walk-away.

Useful evidence: competing offer **at the higher level** at a comparable company; a one-pager of scope (org size, systems owned, outcomes). Years-in-role alone is a weak argument.

Recruiter phrase “you’ll promo in a year” is **not** a contract. Many people stay stuck. Get a **written** promo path only if you still accept the downlevel.

## When accepting a downlevel can be rational

- Team / domain / cash still beat your BATNA and you **accept** the title hit.
- You are exiting a bad situation and have no competing higher-level offer.
- You explicitly want the company brand and will re-level later (know the promo data).

Otherwise: walk or keep negotiating level. Brand pressure is how downlevels stick.
