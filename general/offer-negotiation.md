# Offer negotiation (after you have a written offer)

Negotiation is a **short, data-backed dialogue**. It is **not** the same as [downleveling.md](downleveling.md): **fix level first**, then band, then sign-on / start date. Accepting cash to paper over a lower level trades title, promo clock, and scope.

2024–2026: FAANG packages are off 2021 peaks (mostly equity, not base); **asking is expected again**. AI-lab / senior ML seats can still outrun big-tech cash. Do not invent competing offers.

## Comp pieces (compare apples)

| Component | What it is | Flex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Base** | Recurring cash | Some at startups; tight bands at FAANG |
| **Sign-on** | Year-1 cash; often **clawback** if you leave early | Usually negotiable; different budget than base |
| **Equity (RSU / options)** | Multi-year vest; value moves with price / 409A | Highest lever at public big tech |
| **Bonus target** | % of base; rating-dependent | Often fixed by level |
| **Refresh** | New grants after year 1–2 so TC does not cliff | **Ask the policy** — missing from many verbal pitches |
| **Benefits** | 401(k) match, leave, healthcare | Smaller $; still real |

Public RSUs are marked at **today’s** price. Private equity is illiquid — big tech often **discounts** startup paper heavily. Vesting (cliff vs monthly) changes year-1 cash.

**Level and location** set the ceiling more than your current salary. Companies pay **cost of labor** (market for the skill in that locale), not your personal cost of living.

## Before the offer (recruiter screen)

- In salary-history-ban regions they cannot require current TC. Even elsewhere, **do not anchor on a low current number**.
- Prefer: role first; if pressed, a **market range** from aggregate data (Levels.fyi-style bands for level + metro), not Blind gossip about one peer.
- Get **level** in writing as soon as it is known.

## Once there is an offer

1. **Written breakdown** — base, sign-on, grant + vest, refresh, bonus %, location, **level**. Do not verbally accept to “unlock” the PDF.
2. **Time** — thank them; 5–7 business days is a normal review window. Exploding 24-hour deadlines: ask for alignment with other late-stage loops; artificial scarcity is a culture signal.
3. **One counter** — specific numbers, one or two levers, why you want *this* team. Email is fine; recruiters need a packet they can take upstairs.
4. **Leverage** — a **real** competing offer (company + TC + level) moves ranges; market percentiles are the fallback. Never fabricate.
5. **Final PDF** — sign only what matches the verbal close.

## What to push (typical)

- FAANG: **RSU** and **sign-on** more than base; high end of recruiter approval is often well above first number.
- Startup: base + equity **together**; treat early options as high-variance.
- Always: “What is the **refresh** cadence and typical size at this level?”

## Common mistakes

- Accepting on the first call
- Negotiating cash **before** level is settled
- Apologizing for asking (it is the job)
- Only fighting base
- Citing a single Blind post instead of a level/metro band
- Burning the bridge on a decline — you may re-interview in a few years
