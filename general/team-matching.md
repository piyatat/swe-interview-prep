# Team matching (post-bar placement)

After some companies say **you cleared the bar**, you still need a **manager with headcount** who wants you. That phase is **team matching**. It is not a second coding onsite. Distinct from [recruiter-screen.md](recruiter-screen.md) (triage) and [hiring-manager.md](hiring-manager.md) (often *inside* the loop for a known team).

**Google is the canonical case.** Meta, some AI labs, and new-grad cohorts at other firms run a lighter version. If you applied to a **req tied to one team**, you may skip this and get an offer for that seat.

Ask the recruiter: match **before or after** committee / offer, how long the packet stays live, and whether you can talk to **several teams in parallel**.

## Why it exists

Google (public re:Work): **structured interviews + written feedback**, then people who **never met you** review a packet so no single interviewer hires on vibe. That yields a **generalist hire**. A specific team still has to sponsor the offer.

Consequence: **HC “hire” ≠ job**. No match → no written offer, even if every round was strong.

## Typical Google sequence (2026 guides)

1. Onsite feedback → packet → **Hiring Committee** (weekly; hire / no-hire / more info)
2. Recruiter puts you in a **pool** visible to managers with open HC at your level
3. **Team-match chats** (~30–60 min) with hiring managers; senior roles may add a director or 360 with leads
4. Mutual yes → offer draft → [offer-negotiation.md](offer-negotiation.md)

Reported timing: **2–3 weeks** lucky, **2–3 months** in a tight headcount market. Some write-ups cite an **~8 week** recruiter-attention window and a longer formal packet life (**confirm yours** — numbers conflict). New-grad matching is often **cohort / class** timed, not continuous.

Calls are usually **serialized** (one team at a time). Slow replies kill momentum.

## What the manager is scoring

Not a redo of DSA. Same close-ability as an HM screen, plus **domain appetite**.

| Signal | How it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| Ramp | You can talk about *their* stack/problems after 20 min of homework |
| Enthusiasm | Specific product interest vs “any Google team” |
| Level fit | Scope stories match the headcount they have (L3 vs L5 work) |
| Collaboration | Cross-functional examples; on-call / hours questions asked *by you* |
| Two-way | You evaluate reorg risk, promotion path, manager tenure |

Rare: a light technical probe if the team is specialized. Treat the hour as a **formal interview**, not coffee.

## How to run the call

**Your 90 s:** recent project → the kind of problems you want next → why *this* team (one concrete product or infra bet).

**Questions worth asking** (pick 6, not 20):

- Biggest technical problem this quarter; what “good” looks like in 6 months
- On-call, staffing mix (junior vs senior), last reorg
- Promotion path **on this team** (not Google-in-general)
- Manager tenure; how they run planning and feedback

**Do:** research the product + manager; ask the recruiter for **teams currently matching**; stay flexible on product area if location/level is the constraint. Internal transfer after ~12 months is a common plan B — say it without sounding like a tourist.

**Don’t:** wait passively for inbound; reject the first solid match hoping for Maps/YouTube while the packet cools; recycle a generic “I love scale” pitch.

## If you do not match

Candidacy can **expire**. Re-interview is the usual reset; tell future recruiters you already cleared HC once — some loops shorten. Before reapplying, sharpen a **specialty** so managers can picture a seat.

Competing offer: tell the recruiter **dates**, not a bluff. Matching can accelerate when a team is afraid to lose you.
